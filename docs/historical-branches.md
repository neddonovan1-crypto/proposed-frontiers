# Historical provenance for the alternate chronicle

Research checked 27 September 2026. The game begins in January 1916 and invents consequences through 1939. Sources below establish real institutions, pressures, policies, and biographies; they do **not** establish the game's invented treaties, wars, appointments, casualty totals, or individual outcomes. Examples are research guidance, not a claim that every example is implemented.

## Ibadi government and neighboring Oman

**Real context.** Oman's interior imamate was revived in 1913, before the game's aftermath. The coastal sultanate and interior imamate were competing political authorities; this was not a simple conflict between different religions. The 25 September 1920 Seeb agreement provided reciprocal noninterference, safe commerce and travel, and a 5% ceiling on charges affecting goods coming from the interior to coastal towns. Its practical accommodation should not be confused with an uncontested settlement of external sovereignty.

- [Seeb agreement, British Library record, folio 61r](https://qdl.qa/en/archive/81055/vdc_100070535087.0x00007f) — primary treaty text, including the roles of Isa bin Salih and Imam Muhammad bin Abdullah al-Khalili.
- [English treaty transcription in a later UN dossier](https://www.agda.ae/en/catalogue/tna/fo/371/168698/n/72) — the eight obligations, including commerce and noninterference.
- [British Library overview of Britain and Oman](https://www.qdl.qa/en/close-relationship-britain-and-oman-1750) — institutional and geographic background.
- [1931–32 abdication/accession correspondence](https://www.qdl.qa/en/archive/81055/vdc_100049274833.0x00006d) — Taimur's abdication, Said bin Taimur's accession announced on 10 February 1932, and financial constraints.

**Invented application.** An adjacent player-created Ibadi imamate can prompt correspondence with Nizwa, a recognition dispute, a customs agreement, or pressure from Muscat. Viable access and local consent can support peaceful coexistence. Shared Ibadi identity does not automatically produce union, war, or a population's conversion. A distant state needs a credible travel or diplomatic connection. A new customs pact, its signatories' invented actions, and survival through 1939 remain counterfactual.

## Italy and Mussolini

**Real context.** Italy's 1916 government was not yet Mussolini's regime; he became prime minister in October 1922. Fascist imperial policy subsequently combined infrastructure, propaganda, political coercion, and increasingly explicit racism. Arabic Radio Bari broadcasts began in 1934. Mussolini received the ceremonial “Sword of Islam” in Libya on 18 March 1937 as colonial political theater, not a universally accepted religious office. Italy enacted antisemitic racial laws in 1938, including educational exclusion.

- [Italian presidential archive: 18 March 1937 ceremony](https://archivio.quirinale.it/aspr/gianni-bisiach/AV-002-000487/18-marzo-1937-mussolini-libia-riceve-spada-islam) — archival audiovisual record and description.
- [Original school decree of 5 September 1938, no. 1390](https://www.normattiva.it/atto/caricaDettaglioAtto?atto.codiceRedazionale=038U1390&atto.dataPubblicazioneGazzetta=1938-09-13&tipoDettaglio=originario) — primary legislation, effective 13 September 1938.
- [USHMM: 1938 key dates](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/1938-key-dates) — institutional summary of the racial laws.
- [European Commission: Radio Bari research](https://cordis.europa.eu/article/id/155353-radio-a-propaganda-tool) — Arabic broadcasting and competition with British/French influence.
- [Alexander De Grand, “Mussolini's Follies”](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/contemporary-european-history/article/mussolinis-follies-fascism-in-its-imperial-and-racist-phase-19351940/B385EFFE31E9B62F76BF40886F1388AB) — academic analysis of the imperial and racist turn in 1935–40.

**Invented application.** Italian-held player territory can experience a later change in governing regime, propaganda campaigns, requisitions, censorship, or discriminatory decrees. Direct rule supports a different legal mechanism from an Italian sphere of influence: a local client government faces pressure, not automatic application of every Italian statute. Extending these policies to a newly Italian Levantine or Arabian possession is counterfactual. Do not backdate fascist rule to 1916 or present later wartime deportations as events of 1938–39.

## Ottoman rump state

**Real context.** The revised 1909 Ottoman constitution retained a dynastic sultan-caliph, a cabinet, and a bicameral legislature. Articles 7 and 30 gave parliament roles in treaties and ministerial responsibility; Articles 8 and 17 articulated common Ottoman status and legal equality regardless of religion. These are constitutional provisions, not proof that practice met those commitments. In April 1920 the Allied Supreme Council still contemplated retaining the sultan's government in Constantinople while removing former territories. It explicitly distinguished the proposed Straits Commission from administration of Constantinople itself.

- [FRUS 1909, document 565: revised Ottoman constitution](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1909/d565) — primary constitutional text, especially Articles 2–8, 17, 30, and 42.
- [FRUS 1920, volume III, document 916: San Remo reply](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1920v03/d916) — primary Allied statement, especially points 1, 3, 4, and 8. Its imperial judgments are the participants' positions, not neutral descriptions.
- [FRUS 1924, volume II, document 624](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1924v02/d624) — contemporary US account of Turkish nationalism, military resistance, and replacement of the Sèvres settlement by Lausanne negotiations.

**Invented application.** A territorially viable rump may bargain for constitutional survival, lose authority to a nationalist assembly, or suffer a succession and legitimacy crisis. A surviving postwar Ottoman constitutional monarchy is an alternate outcome, not the historical settlement. Its fortunes should depend on institutions, territory, access, and military pressure rather than the label “Ottoman” guaranteeing either collapse or tolerance.

## Russian sponsorship

**Real context.** The revolutions of 1917 transformed Russian authority. The 3 March 1918 Brest-Litovsk treaty ended Russia's war with the Central Powers; Articles IV and V provided for withdrawal from eastern Anatolia and demobilization. A promise made by imperial Russia in 1916 could not simply continue unchanged through 1939.

- [Brest-Litovsk treaty, Virginia Tech Digital History Reader](https://www.dhr.history.vt.edu/modules/eu/mod03_1917/pdfs/45.pdf) — primary treaty translation, especially Articles I, IV, and V.
- [US National Archives: diplomacy through the Russian Revolution](https://www.archives.gov/publications/prologue/2008/spring/langbart.html) — institutional account of disrupted diplomatic representation and efforts to maintain Russian participation in the war.

**Invented application.** A Russian-sponsored player state faces a broken guarantee, provisional institutions, competing recognition offers, or later negotiations with a different Russian government. None of these outcomes follows automatically from the treaty. Russian, Soviet, local revolutionary, and anti-Bolshevik actors are not interchangeable; later sponsorship needs its own causal explanation.

## Interpretation rules

- Reward consent, enforceable rights, credible guarantees, trade, and workable geography; diversity is not itself a cause of violence.
- Distinguish enacted law, a proposed treaty, an official's claim, and later historical interpretation.
- State-sponsored repression, expulsion, and discrimination have perpetrators and policy mechanisms; do not attribute them to a population's inherent character.
- The newspaper voice is fictional. Sources substantiate historical context, not the generated headlines, exact dates, casualty estimates, or changed biographies.

## January 1916 railway network

Route-specific sources and interruption notes are retained in `railways.json`. [Röll's contemporary inventory](https://eisenbahnwesens.de-academic.com/2548/T%C3%BCrkische), the [Foreign Office Syria and Palestine handbook](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Syria_and_Palestine/Chapter_4), and [Earle's account, pp. 288–289](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/66221/66221-h/66221-h.htm) establish the network and the unfinished Taurus/Amanus transfers. The [July 1916 Kiepert map](https://www.loc.gov/item/2013593013/) is used with these chronological checks, not treated as a January operating timetable. The [German Archaeological Institute study](https://publications.dainst.org/journals/aa/article/view/4366/8635) distinguishes Beersheba's 1915 connection from the later Auja extension and the dismantled Jaffa section.

The map shows regional railway alignments, including the western Anatolian networks and branches, Anatolian main line, disconnected Baghdad Railway sections, Syrian and Palestinian routes, and the Hejaz main line. It does not invent a completed through railway to Baghdad, a Medina–Mecca/Jeddah line, or a pre-1916 Auja extension. Industrial sidings and city tramways are outside the map scale. Operating-route tags follow the playable mask and merged hex geometry; fixed foreign territory cannot be acquired by painting a railway.

The railway articles are counterfactual consequences of the assigned route sections and their political links. Rayak requires its actual junction cell. Route ownership does not imply uniform gauges or a continuous Constantinople–Baghdad train.

## Current game rules and 1939

Turkey with more than 20 hexes and the Ottoman Sultanate with at least 20 fight in 1922. Turkey wins unless the Sultanate has a British or French sphere-of-influence/treaty association; the winner annexes the defeated assignment. These thresholds and the winner rule are explicit game design, not historical estimates. An enlarged Sultanate over 20 hexes otherwise suffers a centralisation revolt. Turkey's regional revolt requires ten majority non-Turkish known-area hexes and at least 30% of its known mapped area in Arabic, Kurdish, Greek or Armenian categories; sparse and mixed areas are excluded. The article attributes conflict to imposed centralisation and repression, not ethnicity itself.

Hellenic Anatolia is a counterfactual territorial name, not a claim that any later Greek administration held the drawn area. The game uses an approximate Anatolian mask compiled into the canonical hex registry. Any assigned hex outside that mask, a grant above thirty hexes, or a proposal without protectorate or direct colonial backing dissolves the administration. A Russian guarantee also fails after the revolution. Greek, British, French and Italian backing lead to distinct treaty, fiscal or basing outcomes. The thirty-hex limit is a game-design threshold, not a historical measure.

The 1939 dispatches consult surviving governments and inherited ports. Their historical anchors are [Germany's occupation of Bohemia and Moravia](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1939v01/d45), [British and French entry into the war](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1939v01/d420), and [Italy remaining outside the war in September 1939](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1939v01/d567). They do not automatically count as wars in the player's score.

Lives Changed contains no invented characters and at most four entries. Candidates now include Mehmed VI, Mustafa Kemal, Faisal, Gertrude Bell, Sulayman al-Baruni and Chaim Weizmann when their geography and political role are relevant. Each has a historical introduction followed by an alternate path. Bell's documented work is grounded in [her Newcastle archive](https://gertrudebell.ncl.ac.uk/l/gb-1-1-2-1-17-19), Faisal's in [the Paris conference record](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1919Parisv05/d1), and Weizmann's in [his conference appearance](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1919Parisv04/d9). The selected entries favour different periods rather than a biography for every territory.

## Second rule edition: administrative viability and the depression

Five adversarial passes used canonical map cells with railway and ethnicity metadata, plus isolated browser saves. They exposed whole-map commons, international commissions, scattered protectorates, tiny guarantors and case-variant names scoring as successful governments.

- Commons containing a marked town or more than 120 cells fail as regional administrations. Modest pastoral commons retain their access benefit but are not states for durability.
- International commissions over 40 cells or four marked towns, or disconnected grants spanning more than four degrees cannot turn a local harbour/holy-city charter into regional sovereignty.
- Governments with more than three components and under 40% of cells in their largest component disintegrate; less extreme fragmentation costs durability. Forty or more administrations of four cells or fewer merge into regional unions. A single administration holding at least 240 cells and cities in three broad regions faces a civil conflict over imposed central taxation, followed by provincial charters. Grants of at least 500 cells covering all four regions dissolve if prior associated autonomies cover less than 20% of the central grant. These are explicit game thresholds, not empirical predictions or demographic risk estimates.
- British and French protectorate finances suffer after 1929; exposure follows associated autonomies too. [The Bank of England](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/about/history) documents Britain's September 1931 gold-standard suspension. [Banque de France research](https://www.banque-france.fr/en/publications-and-statistics/publications/flight-safety-and-credit-crunch-new-history-banking-crisis-france-during-great-depression) documents the French banking contraction of 1930–31. The game's specific subsidy cuts, strikes and renegotiations are invented counterfactual consequences, not documented events in player-created countries.
- Crises, failed grants, military takeovers and financial strain affect scores. Repeated copies of the same agreement are not separate lasting gains. An autonomy cannot retain a dissolved parent in 1939. Scores from earlier rules remain stored separately and cannot dominate the new league edition.
- New assignments use preset names only. Legacy custom names remain readable. Known preset names and historical aliases are normalized. Free Ports require an actual marked port in both the editor and score validation; transfers cannot leave an existing Free Port inland.
