# Persia: historical context and implemented scenarios

Research and implementation checked 2 October 2026 against ruleset 7 in `chronicle.js`. This document describes the implemented branches, not additional proposed features. The shared engine produces browser histories and server-recomputed league scores; `tests/persia.test.cjs` covers the principal boundaries and outcomes.

Persia is a regional patron with limited early military and financial reach. It is not the modern Islamic Republic, and shared Shia identity does not make local scholars or governments subordinate to Tehran. The game permits counterfactual Persian relationships and colonial grants because the player is proposing a different settlement; it does not claim that Persia historically administered these possessions.

## Historical anchors and invented applications

| Period | Documented context | Application invented for the game |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First World War and its aftermath | The Qajar government lacked the finances and forces to impose effective central authority. The Anglo-Persian agreement of 9 August 1919 proposed British advisers, military assistance and finance, while formally affirming Persian independence. [1, 2] | A distant Persian guarantee can fail; the precise supply radius, territorial capacity and 1921 outcomes are balancing rules, not historical measurements. |
| 1920 | Iran did not intervene to support the Iraqi Shia uprising. Later Tehran and Baghdad cooperated against cross-border rebels and the formation of an independent Kurdish state. [3] | Kurdish protection can provoke a dispute over frontier policing. A limited charter offers an alternative outcome; neither outcome is an inevitable ethnic response. |
| 21 February 1921 | Reza Khan's forces captured Tehran. The coup changed the government; the Qajar monarchy remained until 1925. [1] | Local commitments encounter a weak early treasury before a stronger Persian state can make nearby agreements more credible. |
| 1925 onward | The Majles ended Qajar rule on 31 October 1925, and Reza became shah later that year. His administration expanded state authority and reduced autonomous tribal and clerical jurisdictions. [4, 5] | The accession dispatch, frontier missions and competing demands for local autonomy draw on these pressures. Their foreign signatories and negotiated terms are fictional. |
| Interwar religious institutions | Iraqi Shia institutions had their own political interests. Reza Shah also sought to restrict pilgrimage to Iraqi shrines and promoted Qom. [3, 6] | A Shia imamate can preserve clerical institutions through a treaty or reject a sphere of influence. This is not a general loyalty bonus for Shia populations. |
| 1927–28 | Kirkuk's oilfield was discovered in October 1927. [7] | A 1928 Persian-linked Kirkuk government negotiates royalties or contests outside privileges. Persian control of that government and the concession negotiations are counterfactual. |
| 1932–33 | The Anglo-Persian oil dispute reached the League of Nations in December 1932; a revised concession was concluded in April 1933. [8] | A Persian sphere can encounter a credit dispute, and rival missions can clash over Basra navigation. These extensions to player-created states did not occur as described. |
| 4 and 8 July 1937 | Iran and Iraq signed a frontier treaty on 4 July; Iran, Iraq, Turkey and Afghanistan signed the Saadabad non-aggression pact on 8 July. Navigation, river boundaries and Abadan anchorage were important issues. [8, 9, 10] | The Shatt convention and Tehran pact use the player's surviving governments, not the historical signatory list. They require viable, peaceful conditions. The pact promises consultation and non-aggression, not automatic military rescue. |
| 1939 | Iran adopted neutrality after war began in Europe. An October 1939 US diplomatic report describes both that policy and uncertainty over the Saadabad pact. [8, 11] | A September newspaper dispatch has one surviving Persian partner review military transit. The partner's decision is fictional; the game makes no promise that neutrality remains successful beyond 1939. |

## Inputs, derived variables and thresholds

All thresholds below are explicit gameplay choices. They are not estimates of a real Persian expeditionary force, administrative capacity, population share or diplomatic probability.

| Variable | Exact implementation |
| --- | --- |
| Active initial Persian link | `power === 'Persia'` and either direct colonial administration, protectorate or autonomous territory; alternatively, an independent state or imamate with `Treaty association` or `Sphere of influence`. An unused Persia selection with relationship `None` on an independent state or imamate does not activate the branch. |
| Frontier access | `nearPersia` records cell-polygon contact with the mapped Persian frontier. Longitude or a broad centroid-distance approximation alone does not qualify. A cell marked `city === 'Basra'` is also a supply origin. Other marked ports are not automatically Persian bases. |
| `persianReach` | Breadth-first traversal through the supplied set of Persian-linked cells, starting at every frontier/Basra cell, extending at most **six neighbour edges** from an origin. A route may cross another included Persian partner, but not unassigned or foreign-linked cells. The return value is the reached-cell count divided by the territory's total cell count, not an area-weighted share. |
| `supported` | Reach is **at least 0.75**. Exactly 75% qualifies. The initial reach is calculated across eligible surviving Persian states before their individual branches. A subsequent withdrawal cascade removes routes through partners that leave; the late pact also recalculates reach using retained Persian partners. |
| Military dependencies | Only initial protectorates and directly administered colonies count against shared capacity. Autonomous and commercial relationships do not. |
| `persianOverstretched` | **More than four** military dependencies, or **more than 80** cells across those dependencies. Four and 80 are permitted. This triggers withdrawal from all affected protectorates; it also changes the revolt explanation for supported direct colonies. |
| `kurdish` | The sum of mapped Kurdish values at ethnicity index 2 divided by the sum of finite, positive values in the first five named ethnicity entries is **at least 0.35**, and that denominator is positive. Mixed/unknown entries after the first five are excluded. A country's name cannot activate this condition. This is mapped ethnicity coverage, not a population census. |
| `shia` | The government is explicitly an `Imamate` with `sect === 'Shia'`. Shia map coverage alone does not activate this institutional branch. |
| `sphere` | `relationship === 'Sphere of influence'`. |
| `persianQuiet` | No event of kind `war` or `uprising` affects the state from **1930 through 1937 inclusive**. This does not mean that every earlier crisis, war or revolution is absent. |
| Regional failure | The existing rule for **more than three proposed imamates** overrides positive outcomes, suppresses the late Persian accords and zeros the score. This is a game rule, not a historical claim about a denomination or number of governments. |
| Durability | A scoring multiplier, normally 1 unless a branch sets or caps it. It is not a survival probability. Other geographic, governmental and conflict branches can change the final result. |

## Initial if/then branches

These rows are evaluated in order, and only the first matching initial branch fires. The common precondition is an eligible Persian-linked state that has survived preceding special-state checks. The Jewish National Home's incompatible holy-city grant follows its existing special rules instead.

| Priority | If | Event and year | Immediate result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Special case | A valid Hellenic Anatolia grant has a Persian protectorate or colonial guarantee | **Persian Guarantee Fails in Anatolia**, 1922; dissolution | State ends; Persian connection removed. Existing Hellenic limits run first: any cell outside Anatolia or more than 30 cells has its own collapse branch. An unsupported status also fails before this Persia-specific case. |
| 1 | Unsupported direct colony | **Persian Colonial Grant Abandoned**, 1921; dissolution | State ends; district successor governments replace it; power and relationship cleared. |
| 2 | Unsupported protectorate/autonomous territory, or any protectorate when shared capacity is exceeded | **Tehran Withdraws an Unfunded Guarantee**, 1921; crisis | An independent sovereign state survives; power and relationship cleared; durability **0.80**. |
| 3 | Any other unsupported Persian link | **Distant Persian Treaty Reduced to Commercial Terms**, 1922; accord | Government/status preserved; formal alignment cleared; durability **0.95**. Ordinary commerce remains narrative context, not a retained game relationship. |
| 4 | Supported direct colony | **Persian Governors Driven Out by Provincial Revolt**, 1924; uprising | Sovereign republic; power and relationship cleared; durability **0.75**. All supported direct colonies take this branch: the rule does not calculate a probability of revolt. |
| 5 | Supported Kurdish protectorate within capacity | **Kurdish Assembly Rejects Persian Military Conditions**, 1926; crisis | Independent state; power and relationship cleared; durability **0.85**. |
| 6 | Supported Kurdish arrangement that is not a sphere and did not match an earlier row | **Kurdish Frontier Charter Limits Tehran’s Powers**, 1926; accord | Existing government and Persian connection retained; no direct durability change. This row precedes the Shia-imamate row. |
| 7a | Supported Shia imamate in a sphere | **Shia Councils Defend Their Independence from Tehran**, 1927; crisis | Imamate preserved; Persian connection cleared; durability **0.85**. |
| 7b | Supported Shia imamate with a treaty, not already handled above | **Shrine Schools Secure an Independent Endowment Charter**, 1927; accord | Imamate and Persian connection preserved; no direct durability change. Ownership of Najaf is not required by this predicate. |
| 8 | Supported remaining sphere | **Persian Concession Dispute Freezes Treaty Credits**, 1932; crisis, with settlement described in 1933 | Government and Persian connection retained; durability **0.90**. |
| 9 | Supported remaining autonomous territory | **Persian Treaty Recognises the Elected Assembly**, 1926; accord | Government and Persian connection retained; no direct durability change. |
| 10 | Supported remaining protectorate | **Persian Guarantee Secures the Eastern Frontier**, 1926; accord | Protectorate retained; durability **0.95**. |
| 11 | Any remaining supported link | **Persian Frontier Trade Convention Ratified**, 1926; accord | Government and Persian connection retained; no direct durability change. |

After the initial branches, the engine repeatedly recalculates supply without partners that have left the Persian alignment. A still-linked state whose reached-cell share falls below **0.75** receives **Persian Supply Corridor Closes After a Neighbour’s Withdrawal**, a crisis dated one year after the first recorded withdrawal that breaks its route, capped at **1929**. Its Persian power and relationship are cleared. Protectorates and autonomous territories become independent states with durability capped at **0.80**; other governments keep their status with durability capped at **0.90**. Any of that state's single-state accord/crisis events from the withdrawal year onward are removed. The recalculation repeats, so a lost corridor can strand another partner in turn.

The 1925 accession dispatch includes connections with no recorded exit or an exit later than 1925. Thus a Shia sphere that ends in 1927 still participates in the accession story. The engine assembles a chronology from conditions and then sorts its events; it does not advance a separate world state one year at a time. Event years must therefore not be mistaken for the evaluation order above.

## Geographic and late follow-on events

| If | Event | Effect and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The actual marked Basra owner remains intact and Persian-linked, and another intact British-linked state owns Baghdad or shares a cell boundary with Basra's state | **British and Persian Missions Confront Each Other at Basra**, 1933; crisis | All participating states' durability is capped at **0.90**. A French neighbour or an unrelated British coast does not qualify. This is a crisis, not an automatic declaration of war. |
| The actual marked Kirkuk owner remains intact and Persian-linked, with an initial protectorate status or sphere relationship | **Tehran’s Oil Demands Divide the Kirkuk Cabinet**, 1928; crisis | Durability capped at **0.90**; concession delay recorded. A country merely named Kirkuk is insufficient. |
| The same surviving Kirkuk connection has neither of those imposed arrangements | **Kirkuk Assembly Reserves the Oil Royalties**, 1928; accord | No direct durability change. It is not one of the six events that separately qualifies for the Persian cooperation award. |
| No regional failure; Persian Basra survives; Basra, previously identified British river partners, and Baghdad's owner if present all remain intact, have current power Britain/Persia, and satisfy `persianQuiet` | **Shatt Navigation Convention Opens the Gulf**, 1937; accord | Sets the river-settlement flag. It makes no direct durability adjustment. A surviving unrelated-power Baghdad prevents this convention. With no British rival or Baghdad owner, a qualifying Persian Basra can still receive the convention. |
| No regional failure; a retained Persian partner has recalculated reach at least **0.75**, directly contains a frontier/Basra origin, is outside a sphere, is not a military government, has durability at least **0.85**, and satisfies `persianQuiet`; a Basra state with British rivals must also have the river settlement | **Tehran Pact Confirms the Eastern Frontiers**, 1937; accord | Each qualifying state gains **0.03** durability, capped at **1.00**. A supplied inland partner without its own frontier/Basra origin cannot join. A later war or uprising in 1930–37 excludes its affected state. |
| At least one Persian partner remains intact and linked in the late surviving set | **Iran Declares Neutrality; Treaty Partners Review Military Passage**, September 1939; dispatch | One deterministically selected partner supplies the story's local perspective. No direct score change or entry into the European war. |

A Reza Shah biography can also be selected for the newspaper's people section. Its first paragraph states historical background; its second describes the player's counterfactual arrangement. Selection competes with other relevant biographies, so it is not guaranteed on every page.

## Scoring and newspaper presentation

Persia uses the existing score categories: peace up to 40, area-weighted durability up to 40, and cooperation up to 20. It does not receive an unconditional patron bonus.

- **Peace:** `max(0, 40 - 12 × wars - 6 × counted uprisings - 3 × distinct crisis keys - 8 × dissolutions)`. The Persian direct-rule revolt counts as an uprising. Multiple instances of the same crisis title cost three points collectively; dissolutions count per event. The existing specified revenue-revolt and military-coup revolutions also count as uprisings for this formula.
- **Durability:** each input territory contributes its cell-area weight multiplied by its surviving government's final durability; dissolved or unconstituted territory contributes zero. The result is `round(40 × lasting area / all assigned area)`. Persian 0.80, 0.85, 0.90 and 0.95 values and the late 0.03 improvement therefore affect the aggregate through territorial area.
- **Cooperation:** the six eligible Persian events form **one shared five-point award**, capped with other cooperation at 20. They are the frontier trade convention, elected-assembly treaty, Kurdish frontier charter, independent endowment charter, Shatt navigation convention and Tehran pact. At least one event must have every affected state intact, and at least one affected state must still have Persia as its final power. Several such treaties do not stack additional Persian awards.
- **Failure:** no surviving constituted states, no states created, or the existing regional-failure flag produces zero across all score categories.

The news hierarchy places wars, dissolutions and annexations above uprisings/revolutions, then crises, then diplomatic and prosperity stories. Weights and impact distinguish stories within those tiers, with period/geographic variety used for subsequent selections. Thus a Persian colonial collapse or an actual later war leads the newspaper over a Pahlavi accession or treaty dispatch. The Basra crisis cannot invent an interstate war headline without a separate war event. Events after a government's terminal end, and positive events in its terminal year, are removed by the shared chronology cleanup.
